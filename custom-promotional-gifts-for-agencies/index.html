A Supplier Workflow That Starts With the Campaign Brief

The product is only one part of an agency project. The idea must fit the audience, branding must survive production, the budget must include packaging and freight, and every approval must happen before the event date. Flexible Support Across the Campaign

Product discovery

Browse the promotional product catalog or send a theme. We can narrow drinkware, bags, stationery, apparel, event items and packaging based on quantity and timing.

Mockups and artwork review

Digital visuals help your team review placement and color direction. Before production, the final artwork, imprint dimensions and decoration method are confirmed against the selected item.

What to Include in an Agency Product Brief

Even a rough brief is enough to start. These details let us exclude unsuitable products quickly and return options that are easier for your team to compare.

Campaign and audienceWho receives the item, where it will be used and what action or feeling the campaign should support.
Quantity and budgetThe working quantity, target product cost or total budget, and whether more than one tier is required.
Brand requirementsLogo files, brand colors, mandatory artwork, preferred materials and any restricted products or decoration methods.
Timing and destinationsThe event or in-hands date, delivery country, number of locations and whether individual pack-outs are needed.

Keep client decisions visible

Compare Complete Options, Not Isolated Unit Prices

A useful agency comparison should show the product material and size, decoration method, imprint area, MOQ, sample route, packaging, production timing and estimated packed dimensions. This makes the consequences of each choice clear. A cheaper product may require more units; a premium box may increase freight; a complex logo treatment may add approval time.

Control artwork versions

Name the final files clearly and record who approved them. Multi-sponsor projects need one confirmed logo lockup and an owner for late artwork changes.

Confirm the delivered plan

Separate product cost, decoration, packaging and delivery assumptions. If destinations are not final, state which costs are provisional so the client understands what can change.

Prepare a fallback

For fixed-date campaigns, identify which element is flexible: product color, decoration, packaging or freight method. A realistic fallback protects the event without weakening the core idea.

Agency FAQ

Questions Before You Present Options

Can you help when the agency only has a theme?

Yes. Share the audience, setting, quantity, budget and deadline. Reference images and brand guidelines are helpful, but a finished product list is not required.

Can we build multiple price tiers for a proposal?

Yes. Options can be grouped by budget or recipient tier. Keep in mind that MOQ, decoration and packaging may differ between tiers.

Can several products be packed together?

Yes. Before confirming a kit, we review product dimensions, compatibility, protection, insert requirements and the box format so the packed result is practical.

How should we plan for a fixed event date?

Provide the required arrival date and destination at the beginning. The product shortlist should be built around production and shipping feasibility, with approval time included.
