Employee Onboarding Kit Ideas by Budget and Work Style

A strong onboarding kit helps new hires feel prepared before the first meeting, especially when teams are remote or hybrid.

Published by FY PromoGifts | New hire gift planning

A strong onboarding kit helps a new hire feel prepared before the first meeting. It also gives HR and people teams a practical way to make the company’s values visible. The best kits are not simply boxes of branded merchandise: they are designed around the employee’s first week, work setting and delivery method.

Start with three questions: What will the employee actually use? Which items need to arrive before day one? Will the kits go to one office or to individual home addresses? Those answers determine the product mix, packaging and realistic budget more clearly than a long wish list.

Build the kit around the first working week

Useful items earn their place because they solve a small, immediate need. A notebook and pen support orientation sessions. A tumbler or water bottle can be used at a desk. A tote or pouch organizes equipment. A simple card explains why each item was chosen and can point the new hire toward internal resources.

A balanced standard kit might contain one hero item, two everyday support items and one welcome insert. For example, pair insulated drinkware with a notebook, pen and printed card. For a more compact mailer, use a notebook, cable organizer, sticker sheet and folding tote. You can explore more combinations on the employee onboarding kits page or test a mix in the Kit Studio.

Choose products by work setting

Office-based teams can receive desk items, drinkware, access-card holders, tote bags and stationery in bulk at one location. This makes larger or heavier products easier to manage. The kit can be placed at each desk or distributed during orientation.

Remote employees need products that travel well and packaging that protects the contents. Compact tech accessories, notebooks, drinkware with secure lids, pouches and soft goods are usually easier to ship than fragile or oversized pieces. Ask for a packed-size estimate before finalizing the mix, because carton dimensions can change delivery costs.

Hybrid teams benefit from a consistent core kit with an optional location-specific item. Keeping most components the same protects brand consistency and simplifies reordering.

Plan practical budget tiers

A tier should reflect a genuine difference in recipient or program goal, not just add more items. A useful structure is:

  • Essential kit: notebook, pen and welcome card for large-volume hiring or temporary teams.
  • Core kit: one hero product such as a tumbler, plus two workday items and coordinated packaging.
  • Leadership or specialist kit: upgraded materials, an additional tech or travel item, and more refined presentation.

If several tiers are required, share the quantities for each one. Some products have different minimum order quantities, so a premium item that works for 200 employees may not be practical for a group of 12. Selected products support lower quantities, but MOQ always depends on the item, logo method and packaging.

Remote delivery requires a better packing plan

When a kit is shipped directly to a home, the outer package becomes part of the experience. It should protect the contents without wasting space. A custom printed box is one option, but a stock mailer with a branded sleeve, sticker or insert can create a cohesive result at a lower setup cost.

Confirm whether address labels will be applied by the supplier, a local fulfillment partner or your own team. If recipient data will be shared, decide who can access it and when it must be deleted. For international lists, check country restrictions and delivery estimates before promising one global arrival date.

Keep artwork readable and the kit coherent

Not every item needs the largest possible logo. Use a strong logo treatment on one or two products and let color, packaging and the welcome card connect the rest. Vector artwork such as AI, EPS, SVG or print-ready PDF gives the production team the clearest starting point. Brand guidelines should include approved colors and minimum logo size where available.

Before bulk production, confirm product color, decoration method, imprint size, packaging layout, insert text and final quantities. A digital mockup helps stakeholders review placement, while a physical sample may be useful when material, color matching or a complex pack-out is critical.

Create a repeatable onboarding program

If hiring continues throughout the year, choose products that can be reordered and document the approved specification. Record product codes, colors, logo files, decoration details, box size and insert version. Decide whether you will place scheduled batches or hold stock. A repeatable core kit is easier to manage than rebuilding the project for every new employee.

What to send when requesting a quote

  • Total quantity and any separate employee tiers.
  • Target budget per kit, including or excluding delivery.
  • Required arrival date and destination countries or postal codes.
  • Office bulk delivery versus individual home addresses.
  • Preferred products, colors, packaging and logo files.
  • Whether a digital mockup or physical sample is needed.

A complete brief lets the supplier identify unsuitable items early and build the quote around the real deadline. If some details are unknown, send the audience, quantity, budget and date first; product recommendations can follow from those constraints.

Employee onboarding kit FAQ

How many items should an onboarding kit include?

Three to five coordinated pieces are often enough. One useful hero product, two supporting items and a welcome card generally feel more intentional than many low-value objects.

Can the same kit work for remote and office employees?

Yes, if the core products are compact and durable. You can keep the core consistent and adapt the outer packaging or one location-specific item.

How early should we start?

Start as soon as the hiring date, quantity and destinations are known. Timing varies by product, decoration, sample approval, packaging and shipping route, so the arrival date should be included in the first request.
